# How I learn

> [!example] This is a fake note about a made-up person
> It exists to show what a filled-in About Me note looks like. **Delete it.**

## The short version

By breaking something and fixing it. Never by reading first.

## What actually works

- **Doing it wrong once.** I don't retain anything from a tutorial I followed
  successfully. I retain everything from the thing that broke at step 4.
- **One fix at a time.** If I'm given eight troubleshooting steps I'll do all
  eight at once, it'll work, and I'll have no idea which one did it.
- **Being shown, then left alone.** Watching someone do it once is worth an hour
  of explanation. Watching them do it twice is worth nothing.

## What doesn't

- **Video.** I can't skim it and I can't search it. I'll watch 20 minutes and
  retain the thumbnail.
- **Being taught the theory first.** I need a reason to care about the theory,
  and the reason is always a problem I already hit.
- **Long explanations before the answer.** I stop reading at the third paragraph
  and scroll for a code block.

## What this means for an AI

- Hand me the working thing. Explain it underneath if it's interesting.
- One fix at a time when something is broken. Wait for me to report back.
- If I ask "why", *then* give me the theory — that's the signal that I've hit the
  problem the theory answers.
